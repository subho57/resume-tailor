About the role
The best engineers aren’t slotted into a pillar on day one. They’re given the highest-impact problem on the platform and expected to work it with rigour. Generalist-first, specialist-later. That’s the model here.

Core engineering sits at the centre of the Shipsy flywheel. Forward Deployed Engineers prove patterns inside a customer. Product leaders frame the long-term bets. Your job is to turn those signals into platform — productising what deserves to be productised, and building new primitives where the platform is thin.

The pillars you could work on are: AgentFleet (the agent orchestration layer), AgentFlow (the workflow runtime), the TMS and WMS cores, the routing and optimisation engine, the integration fabric, and the data platform underneath all of it. Where you start depends on where you fit and where the leverage is — not on a slot we filled six months ago.

What you’ll own
Module-level design. Given a problem, you own the design, the build, the tests, the deploy, the on-call. No hand-off to a platform team on the other side of the building — you are the platform team.
Production code at scale. Everything you ship runs against 5 billion+ shipments a year. Performance, correctness, and observability are not afterthoughts — they are the job.
Productising the FDE flywheel. A Forward Deployed Engineer proves something inside one customer. You take the pattern, generalise it, and make it a platform primitive that works for the next ten.
API and interface design. Integration partners, FDEs, and customers use the surfaces you design. Clean, stable, well-documented APIs are the bar — not the aspiration.
Code review and pattern-setting. We’re small enough that every engineer reviews every engineer. The standards of the codebase are what you and your peers decide they are.
Scaling yourself. Use Claude, GPT, and agent frameworks aggressively in your own workflow. The person on the team who has automated more of their own work than anyone else wins.
Picking your pillar. After your first six months, you’ll have seen enough of the platform to decide where you want to go deep. Generalist-first, specialist-later.
Who we’re looking for
We’re not hiring a specialist in any single stack. We’re hiring an engineer with strong foundations who can be thrown at any part of the platform and be productive inside a fortnight.

2–4 years of production engineering. You’ve shipped code to real users at meaningful scale — not a single internship or a coursework project.
Tier-1 education. IIT / NIT / BITS / equivalent, or a top international institution.
Strong fundamentals. Data structures, algorithms, systems thinking. You can design a schema, reason about a distributed failure, and instrument a slow endpoint without a runbook.
Generalist instincts. You’ve worked across backend, frontend, infra, or data and can move between them. No “I only do backend.”
High agency. You’ve picked a project, owned it end-to-end, and shipped it without a PM spec pushing you. Ideally you’ve also built something outside work — a side project, open source, or a company.
You use AI tools in your work. Hands-on with Claude, GPT, and agent frameworks. We’ll ask what you’ve actually built, not what you’ve read about.
Taste. You can tell the difference between a 100-line function that’s clean and a 100-line function that’s a maintenance nightmare — and you can explain why.
Where the role sits
You’re on the Core Platform team, based out of Gurugram. Your first rotation is assigned based on where you fit and where the highest-leverage problem is right now. After six months you can request a pillar move, or you can stay and go deep — your call.

You pair frequently with Forward Deployed Engineers. They see the customer pain first, you productise the fix. It’s a closed feedback loop, on purpose — the engineers who do this well become the ones who shape the next version of the platform.

You report to a Staff Engineer and have a skip-level with engineering leadership. No manager overhead. Progression is sharp: strong junior → Senior Software Engineer in 18–24 months → Staff Engineer with a pillar of your own, typically in 3–4 years. The ceiling is what you can hold, not what the title ladder allows.
