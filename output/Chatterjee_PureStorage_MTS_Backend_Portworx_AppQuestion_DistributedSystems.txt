At Turbot HQ, I built a Kafka-based event-driven pipeline where multiple consumers asynchronously processed policy-enforcement events across the system, with a dead-letter queue for reliable failure handling and reprocessing when downstream steps failed. I also designed a REST API microservice in Go that operates as a gRPC client talking to several other internal microservices, so getting service-to-service contracts and failure handling right across that boundary was part of the design from the start. Separately, I resolved a production incident where a Go service was stuck in a Kubernetes CrashLoopBackOff — diagnosing a distributed-system failure mode (a service repeatedly failing health checks and restarting) by digging into pod logs and status rather than trusting the surface signal, and working with the platform team to restore it. And on the data side, I engineered Tailpipe, a Go-based observability CLI, to handle high-throughput distributed log ingestion — chunking and batching reads and streaming writes to sustain 1,000+ logs per second without running out of memory.
